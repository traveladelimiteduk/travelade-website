Most Schengen visa applications require the same core set of documents. The exact list varies slightly by country and purpose of travel. We send a tailored checklist once we know your itinerary — including any extras your specific destination requires.

1

The Core Documents

Nine documents cover almost every standard UK application. Each card lists what the document must actually show — and the single mistake that most often causes problems with it.

1. Valid Passport

Issued within the last 10 years. Must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended return date. Must have at least 2 blank pages for the visa sticker.

Common mistake: Applying with a passport that expires within 6 months of the return date — this alone is grounds for refusal.

2. Application Form

Completed fully, signed by the applicant, and dated within 15 days of your appointment. Every field must be answered — leave nothing blank. If a question does not apply, mark it N/A.

Common mistake: Names on the form not matching the passport exactly, including middle names and hyphenated surnames.

3. Passport Photos

Two recent colour photographs, 35×45mm, white background, face centred, taken within the last 6 months. Check the exact specification for your destination before printing.

Common mistake: Photos taken more than 6 months ago, or with a coloured/grey background instead of white.

4. Travel Insurance

Minimum €30,000 medical cover. Must be valid across all Schengen countries for the entire trip duration — ideally a few days either side to cover delays and missed connections.

Common mistake: Insurance that only covers one country, or does not span the full trip dates including travel days.

5. Proof of Accommodation

Hotel bookings, rental confirmation, or a host invitation letter covering every night of your stay. Must align exactly with your flight dates — no gaps.

Common mistake: Airbnb screenshots or unconfirmed listings. Use the official booking confirmation PDF with your name on it.

6. Proof of Finances

3 months of bank statements showing regular income and a stable balance. The benchmark is roughly £90 per person per day. An original bank letter is required alongside the statements.

Common mistake: A large deposit made just before applying — consulates treat this as funds deposited for the application, not regular savings.

7. Travel Itinerary

Round-trip flight reservation and a day-by-day schedule that is consistent with your accommodation bookings. A provisional booking is acceptable — you do not need to have purchased flights.

Common mistake: Flight dates that do not match hotel check-in/check-out, leaving overnight gaps the consulate cannot account for.

8. Employment / Status Proof

Employed: payslips and an employer letter confirming role, salary and approved leave. Self-employed: business registration and bank statements. Retired: pension statements. Student: enrolment letter.

Common mistake: Employer letters on plain paper rather than company headed paper, or missing the confirmed return-to-work date.

9. Cover Letter

Optional but strongly recommended. A short letter explaining the purpose of your visit, the dates, and the itinerary. It ties every other document together and gives the officer context.

Common mistake: Skipping it entirely. When documents are borderline, a well-written cover letter often makes the difference.

Which Country to Apply Through

You must apply to the consulate of the Schengen country where you will spend the longest part of your stay. If two countries share equal nights, apply through the one you enter first. Getting this wrong means starting over with a new appointment and new fees.

Appointment Requirements

First-time applicants must attend in person at a VFS Global or TLScontact centre in the UK to give biometrics (fingerprints and a digital photograph). Appointments must be booked in advance and are often the longest wait in the process.

1

Book your appointment

Use the VFS Global or TLScontact portal for your destination country. Slots fill weeks ahead — book as soon as your travel dates are confirmed. We can do this on your behalf.

2

Prepare all documents

Arrive with originals and one clear copy of every document. Some centres require documents in a specific order. Bring the appointment confirmation email and a valid photo ID.

3

Give your biometrics

Fingerprints and a digital photograph are captured at the centre. This takes 10–20 minutes. You will leave your passport with the centre — do not plan travel requiring it until the visa is returned.

4

Pay the visa fee

The Schengen visa fee (currently €90 for adults, €45 for children 6–11) is paid at the appointment centre, not to us. VFS/TLS also charge a service fee on top.

5

Wait for the decision

Standard processing is 15 calendar days. Apply at least 6 weeks before travel to allow time to reapply if needed. Some consulates process faster; none guarantee a turnaround.

4

Additional Documents by Purpose

The core nine documents apply to all applicants. Select your travel purpose below to see what else the consulate will expect.

  • Round-trip flight reservation — provisional booking is acceptable.
  • Hotel or accommodation booking for every night of your stay.
  • Day-by-day itinerary — a simple plan showing where you will be each day.
  • Travel insurance with min €30,000 medical cover valid across all Schengen countries.
  • Bank statements (3 months) showing you can fund the trip without financial strain.
  • Invitation letter from the host company in the Schengen country — on headed paper, signed, detailing the nature and duration of the visit.
  • Employer letter from your UK employer confirming your role, salary and that you are authorised to travel for this purpose.
  • Company registration documents if self-employed — Companies House certificate or sole trader registration.
  • Conference or event agenda if attending a specific event — registration confirmation is ideal.
  • Business bank statements if self-employed, showing the business is active.
  • Invitation letter from your host — signed, stating their name, your relationship, address, dates and that they will host you.
  • Proof of host's status — copy of their passport, residence permit or citizenship document for the Schengen country.
  • Proof of relationship — birth certificate, marriage certificate or similar document showing the family connection.
  • Declaration of sponsorship if your host is funding the trip — some consulates require a notarised financial declaration.
  • Children travelling alone need both parents' signed consent if a parent is not travelling.
  • Letter from UK treating doctor confirming the need for treatment abroad and the specific country.
  • Appointment confirmation from the medical facility in the Schengen country — date, type of treatment and estimated duration.
  • Proof of funds for medical costs — a clear statement showing sufficient funds to cover treatment and living expenses.
  • Accommodation covering the full treatment period — hotel or private address near the facility.
  • Accompanying person documents if a carer is travelling with the patient — their own full application plus a letter explaining their role.
  • Enrolment letter from the institution confirming your course, dates and full-time status.
  • Proof of funding — scholarship letter, parental support declaration or bank statements showing sufficient funds for the study period.
  • Accommodation for the full study period — student halls confirmation or a private rental agreement.
  • Academic transcripts — some consulates ask for prior qualification evidence to validate enrolment.
  • Note: If the course exceeds 90 days, a long-stay national visa is required rather than a Schengen short-stay visa. Different rules and forms apply.

Common Reasons for Refusal

Most refusals are preventable. These are the eight reasons we see most often — and how to avoid them.

Reason 01

Insufficient proof of financial means

Bank statements showing too little balance, or funds that were deposited shortly before applying rather than accumulated over time.

Reason 02

Dates not consistent across documents

Insurance, flights and hotel bookings that don't share the same travel dates — even one day's mismatch can fail an application.

Reason 03

Application filed through the wrong country

Applying to France when the longest stay is in Italy. The correct consulate won't accept a file already submitted elsewhere.

Reason 04

Purpose of visit unclear or unsupported

No cover letter, no day-by-day itinerary, or a stated purpose that the other documents don't back up.

Reason 05

Travel insurance does not meet requirements

Cover below €30,000, limited to one country only, or with dates that don't span the entire trip including travel days.

Reason 06

No evidence of ties to the UK

Consulates must be satisfied you intend to return. Without a job, property, family or other ties, this can be hard to demonstrate.

Reason 07

Previous Schengen refusal not disclosed

The application form asks about previous refusals directly. Not disclosing one is treated as misrepresentation, which is a more serious ground than the original refusal.

Reason 08

Incomplete or unsigned application form

Missing signature, blank fields left empty rather than marked N/A, or a name that does not match the passport exactly.
