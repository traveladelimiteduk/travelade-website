The short version

Four things worth knowing before you start

About 15 calendar days

That is how long most applications take once the consulate has your file. Apply three weeks ahead of travel, longer in peak season.

Longest stay wins

You apply through the country where you spend most nights, or where you arrive first if it is an even split. Everything you submit must match.

€30,000 minimum

Travel insurance covering the whole Schengen area for your entire stay. Dates must line up with your flights, with margin at both ends.

One file, one story

Refusals are usually caused by documents disagreeing with each other, not by anything unusual in your circumstances.

Before you apply

Most applications are decided within 15 calendar days of the consulate receiving your file, which works out at roughly 7 to 15 working days once bank holidays and weekends are excluded. Many are decided faster. Consulates may extend this to 45 calendar days where additional checks are needed, and processing slows during peak travel season, so applying at least three weeks before you travel is sensible.

Yes. British citizens need a Schengen visa for every short stay in the Schengen area, including holidays and business trips of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Ireland is the only exception, because it sits outside the Schengen area. A UK residence permit does not replace a Schengen visa.

Apply through the country where you plan to spend the longest stay. If your trip is split evenly between two countries, apply through the one where you arrive first. Your flight bookings, hotel reservations and insurance should all match the country you nominate, because consulates check these documents for consistency.

Documents

Nine core documents cover almost every standard application: a valid passport, a completed and signed application form, two passport photographs, a round-trip flight itinerary, proof of accommodation for every night, three months of bank statements with a supporting bank letter, travel insurance with at least €30,000 of medical cover, a cover letter, and a booked biometric appointment. Children and applicants in specific situations need additional paperwork. Our full checklist page explains what each document must show.

It must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid for at least three months beyond your planned departure date from the Schengen area. You also need at least two blank pages for the visa sticker. Passports with damage, or with previous visa pages removed, are frequently refused, so renew before you apply rather than hoping it is overlooked.

Yes, travel insurance is mandatory for every Schengen visa application. You need a minimum of €30,000 of medical cover, valid across all Schengen countries and covering your entire stay. Cover that starts a few days before departure and ends a few days after you get home removes the most common date mismatch, which is where policies and flights disagree about when you are travelling.

There is no fixed legal minimum, but you must show you can support yourself for the length of your stay. A common benchmark is roughly £90 per person per day, held consistently over the previous three months rather than deposited shortly before you apply. A single-use statement held for a few days is read as newly deposited money, which works against you.

A short letter, usually one side of a page, explaining the purpose of your travel, your dates and your itinerary. It does not need to be persuasive. It needs to be consistent with everything else in the file. If your cover letter describes one trip and your hotel bookings describe another, the inconsistency is what gets noticed.

Every traveller needs their own visa and their own passport, including infants. Children also need a full birth certificate. If they travel without one or both parents, you need consent documentation from any parent who is not travelling, and in some cases a notarised letter.

Appointments and biometrics

You complete the application form online through the destination country's portal, but a first application is not finished online. Biometrics are required in person at a visa application centre, normally within 59 months of a previous set of fingerprints. Some embassies run repeat-applicant schemes, but the initial visit is almost always in person.

Biometrics means fingerprints and a compliant photograph, captured digitally at the visa application centre. First-time applicants must attend in person, as must anyone whose fingerprints are more than 59 months old or whose details such as your name change. Children under 12 usually attend with a parent and may have fingerprints taken at the appointment.

After you submit

At the appointment your documents are submitted and biometrics captured. The centre forwards the file to the consulate, who reviews it. Most decisions are made within 15 calendar days. You either keep the passport and receive a decision letter, or collect the passport and visa sticker from the centre. If you have opted into courier, it comes back to you instead.

Most are issued as multiple-entry visas valid for one or two years, though anything from six months to five years is possible depending on your circumstances and history. The number of entries does not change the 90-day limit: you can still spend at most 90 days in any rolling 180-day period across the whole area.
